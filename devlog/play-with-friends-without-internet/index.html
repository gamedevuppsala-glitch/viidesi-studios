Play with friends without internet, on one Wi-Fi

How Chhatpat's local multiplayer works with no data at all. A 5-digit game ID, up to 4 phones on one Wi-Fi or hotspot, a shared city with everyone's house in it, and a pot of home objects.

In a lot of India, the fun is in the same room and the data pack is not. So play with friends in Chhatpat! works without internet: up to four phones on the same Wi-Fi, or on one friend’s phone hotspot.

How a game starts

  1. One phone taps Host. It shows a 5-digit game ID.
  2. Friends tap Join and type the ID.
  3. Everyone marks ready and the city is built on each phone.

There is no server and no account. The phones talk to each other directly over the local network, so it works at a cousin’s wedding, on a school trip or in a hostel with spotty data. (Bluetooth would have been nice, but it cannot carry a race like this. A hotspot can.)

One city, everyone’s house in it

The city in a match is shared. Every player’s own ghar, the house they have decorated in single player, stands in the same city. You all race the same errand from start to finish, and you can see your friends as ghosts running beside you, with a race strip showing who is ahead.

A pot worth winning

Before the race, each player can put up to three things from their home into the pot: a jhoola, a cooler, a showcase, a matka. The winner takes all of it home. It makes a quick race feel like the gully final.

Why local first

Local play costs players nothing, needs no sign-in and keeps working when the network does not. It also fits how people already play games together here: phones out, sitting next to each other, shouting.
