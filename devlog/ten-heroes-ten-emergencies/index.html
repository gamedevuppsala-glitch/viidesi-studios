Ten heroes, ten desi emergencies

Kamla Dadi's serial, Pappu's baraat, Mummy's pressure cooker and seven more. Meet the ten heroes of Chhatpat and the filmi emergencies that send them running across the rooftops.

A runner needs a reason to run. In Chhatpat! every hero has a small, very Indian emergency and a clock, and every story plays out like a filmi scene. Here is the parivaar.

The ten stories

  • Aarav, “Home Alone”. Riya’s parents are out. For now. 22. BA pass (third attempt). Hopeless romantic.
  • Raju, “10-Minute Delivery”. One order. One address. One very specific item. 4.9 stars. Zero chill. Never checks the package.
  • Golu, “Howzat”. One six. One window. One very angry Sharma Uncle. Age 9. Batting average 3. Window average 100%.
  • Bunty, “Rishta Runner”. Aunty Ji arranged a rishta. The gully final starts in five. Opening batsman. Runaway groom. Still in the sherwani.
  • Kamla Dadi, “Prime Time”. Power cut. Her serial starts in minutes. The neighbour has a generator. 78. Bad knee. Has not missed an episode since 2003.
  • Pappu, “Band Baaja Bhaag”. The baraat can’t move without its dhol. The dhol is Pappu. 300 weddings. One dhol. Zero days off.
  • Sweety, “Golden Hour”. Golden hour lasts twenty minutes. The brand deal needs one selfie. 1.2M followers. 14 ring lights.
  • Mummy, “Cooker ki Seeti”. Three seetis done. The fourth one takes the ceiling. Kitty party queen. Housie champion. Dal in mortal danger.
  • Munna, “Sulabh Sprint”. Sharma Aunty’s rajma. One working toilet. 1.4 km.
  • Tinku, “Forwarded Many Times”. Chacha forwarded a scam to 212 groups. Delete it before Dadi reads it. 19. Family IT department. Unpaid. Unstoppable.

Every hero has a move

Each hero brings a special move to the run, like the Dhol Blast, the Paan Pichkaari or a well-timed “Khana Khaya?”. On top of that come the three power-ups anyone can use: the patang to glide over the roofs, chai to sprint and the gulel to swing up to the highest point ahead.

English or Desi

Every line in the game can be read in plain English or in Desi mode, which is Hinglish full of pop-culture references. Try the English | Desi switch at the top of this site to get a taste.
