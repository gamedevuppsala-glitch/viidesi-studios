How Chhatpat builds a new Indian city every run

Gullies 2.6 metres wide, avenues built to Indian road standards, 400 hand-painted surfaces and a crowd that walks into shops. How Chhatpat's procedural desi city is put together on a phone.

Chhatpat! is a rooftop parkour game, so the city is the level. And because every run builds a new one, the city has to be good every single time: jumpable, readable, and unmistakably desi.

Streets first, sized like real Indian streets

Indian streets are not one width. A run in Chhatpat has four classes, each scaled from Indian urban road standards:

  • Gully: 2.6 metres. You jump these.
  • Lane: 4.5 metres, a 3.5 metre carriageway. Still a roof jump, just.
  • Road: 9.6 metres, two 3-metre lanes and 1.8-metre footpaths.
  • Avenue: 15.6 metres, four lanes with dashes between them.

A straight run keeps one class, and may change by only one step at a junction, so streets flow instead of flickering between sizes. Where a wide street turns into a narrow one, the carriageway tapers and the footpath widens to fill the space. Where two streets with footpaths meet, the kerb sweeps round a 4 to 6 metre radius. Gullies keep their sharp corners, because that is what gullies do.

Buildings that feel lived in

On those blocks go havelis, chawls, kothis, shops and workshops, temples, masjids, gurudwaras and stepwells. Landmarks rise over the roofs so you can find your way: the Red Fort, Qutub Minar, India Gate, Humayun’s Tomb, Jama Masjid, Akshardham, the Lotus Temple and Agrasen ki Baoli.

Every surface is painted. The library holds over 400 Indian surfaces, from jaali screens to Ajrakh block prints, painted with visible brush strokes and sized to real-world scale. One choice of material per building keeps a street from turning into a patchwork.

Roofs are the playground, so they get the most care: water tanks, mumties (the little stair huts on top), washing lines you slide under, charpais, and green pipes that tell you where you can climb.

A street that is busy for a reason

More than 100 kinds of people walk the streets, each with their own walk: chaiwalas, golgappe wale, dabbawalas, shoppers. They do not just stand and pose. Walkers go into open shops and come out again, and people chat in rings with one speaker at a time. Autos and traffic keep to the road’s inner lane, and sky buses and drones fly above.

Built while you look at the menu

A full city takes real work, so Chhatpat builds it behind the start screen. By the time you have picked a hero and an outfit, the city is standing, and the start screen itself is set inside it, with your hero waiting on a roof and the longest open street running away behind them.
